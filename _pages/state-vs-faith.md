  <section class="research-project__section">
    <h2>Overview</h2>
    <p>
      The project asks a simple but important question: when states try to discipline, marginalize, or replace religious authority, do they weaken religious politics or unintentionally create the conditions for long-run backlash?
    </p>
    <p>
      I argue that secularizing interventions can transform religious attachment rather than simply reduce it. In contexts where secularization is experienced as coercive, state-building projects may deepen grievance, reshape collective identity, and alter political responses over time.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-project__section">
    <h2>Research Agenda Fit</h2>
    <p>
      The paper sits at the intersection of religion and politics, public opinion, political behavior, and state formation. More broadly, it contributes to debates about the unintended political consequences of secular governance.
    </p>
